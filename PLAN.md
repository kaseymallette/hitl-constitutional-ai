# Execution Plan

The project follows Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) as the paper did it. The author's work was writing the constitution. The only further input from the author is picking the better of two responses during evaluation.

## Steps

1. **Principles.** The paper used 16 principles, each written as a critique request and a revision request. The constitution has 18 sections (1.1–3.6), so each section becomes one principle, written from the constitution's own wording. The author approves or rejects each pair.
2. **Prompts.** As in the paper: red-team and helpfulness prompts from Anthropic's public dataset (`Anthropic/hh-rlhf` on Hugging Face), plus more prompts generated by a model. The red-team prompts mostly test harm (Article II), so the generated prompts cover Articles I and III.
3. **Stage 1: critique and revision.** The model answers a prompt, critiques its response against a randomly chosen principle, and revises it, for up to 4 rounds. The few-shot examples come from the paper's appendix. This can run locally in Ollama.
4. **Stage 1: supervised fine-tuning.** Fine-tune on the final revisions, in PyTorch.
5. **Stage 2: reinforcement learning from AI feedback.** The Stage 1 model writes two responses per prompt. A model picks which response better follows a principle. Those picks train a preference model, and the Stage 1 model is then trained with PPO against it.
6. **Evaluation.** On held-out prompts, the author picks which of two responses (trained vs. untrained) better follows the constitution, as the paper's human raters did.

## Constraints

- The main Mac is an M2 with 8 GB of memory: enough to generate responses in Ollama, not enough to train a 3–4B model in PyTorch. Training runs on a separate computer.
- Training needs the original Hugging Face weights. The Ollama models are compressed 4-bit copies and can't be fine-tuned.
- The paper trained one model. Starting with one of the three models in the README is faster and closer to the paper.
