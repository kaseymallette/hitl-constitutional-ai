# Execution Plan

The project follows Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) as the paper did it. The author's work was writing the constitution. The only further input from the author is picking the better of two responses during evaluation.

## Steps

1. **Principles.** The paper used 16 principles, each written as a critique request and a revision request. The constitution has 18 sections (1.1–3.6), so each section becomes one principle, written from the constitution's own wording. The author approves or rejects each pair. The pairs are in [PRINCIPLES.md](PRINCIPLES.md).
2. **Prompts.** As in the paper: red-team and helpfulness prompts from Anthropic's public dataset (`Anthropic/hh-rlhf` on Hugging Face), plus more prompts generated by a model. The red-team prompts mostly test harm (Article II), so the generated prompts cover Articles I and III. Every generated prompt is written by the same speaker, described in `data/prompts/speaker.txt`: a single straight woman in her early thirties with no children. The author judges the responses, and for Articles I and III that judgment is often about subjective experience. Section 1.3 makes the user the sole authority over their own experience, so the speaker is kept close to the author's position, and holding the speaker constant keeps it from becoming a variable. The `hh-rlhf` prompts are left as they are.
3. **Stage 1: critique and revision.** The model answers a prompt, critiques its response against a randomly chosen principle, and revises it, for up to 4 rounds. The few-shot examples come from the paper's appendix. This can run locally in Ollama.
4. **Stage 1: supervised fine-tuning.** Fine-tune on the final revisions, in PyTorch.
5. **Stage 2: reinforcement learning from AI feedback.** The Stage 1 model writes two responses per prompt. A model picks which response better follows a principle. Those picks train a preference model, and the Stage 1 model is then trained with PPO against it.
6. **Evaluation.** On held-out prompts, the author picks which of two responses (trained vs. untrained) better follows the constitution, as the paper's human raters did.

## Later experiment: the speaker

A model that sees one speaker might learn the constitution's behaviors for her and not in general. A separate, controlled experiment tests this. It changes one thing about the speaker at a time (gender, sexuality, relationship status, or children), keeps the seeds the same, and compares the same models on each version. It includes Section 3.1 cases where the user talks about a spouse, children, or a date, to see whether the model makes that person the subject. Each version is judged by evaluators who share the speaker's experience.

## Constraints

- Everything runs on one Windows 11 laptop: an AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 (12 cores), 32 GB of memory, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti Laptop GPU with 12 GB of VRAM, and about 790 GB of free disk space. Generation in Ollama and training in PyTorch both run on the NVIDIA GPU.
- 12 GB of VRAM is not enough for full fine-tuning of a 3–4B model, which needs about 16 bytes per parameter (roughly 50–65 GB) for weights, gradients, and optimizer state. Training uses LoRA, or QLoRA (LoRA on a 4-bit base) where memory is tight. This departs from the paper, which fine-tuned all weights.
- PPO in Stage 2 holds the policy, a reference model, a value model, and the preference model at once. With LoRA, the policy and reference share one base model (the reference is the base with the adapter off), and the preference model can also be a LoRA adapter on the same base. Even so, Stage 2 is the tightest step for memory.
- The RTX 5070 Ti is a Blackwell GPU, so it needs a PyTorch build for CUDA 12.8 or later.
- Training needs the original Hugging Face weights. The Ollama models are compressed 4-bit copies and can't be fine-tuned.
- The paper trained one model. Starting with one of the three models in the README is faster and closer to the paper.

## Decisions

- The first model trained is Llama 3.2 3B, using the instruction-tuned weights (`meta-llama/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct` on Hugging Face). The paper started from a model already trained to be helpful, and the Instruct model is the closest match. The same model generates responses in Ollama as `llama3.2:3b`.
- The Llama weights on Hugging Face are gated. Downloading them requires accepting Meta's Llama 3.2 Community License on the model page and signing in with a Hugging Face access token.
